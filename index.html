eltrace

A camera that traces. It works out how things are moving, then stops refreshing the picture — so the motion keeps dragging pixels that should have been replaced. You get the trace of a corrupted video file, live.

Nothing is uploaded. The picture, the clips and the stills never leave this device.

Three things, then you're shooting

  1. Pick a filter from the row above the shutter. 0 Off is the plain picture.
  2. Freeze, on the right, stops the picture refreshing while motion keeps dragging it. That is the effect the whole app is built on.
  3. The big button takes a photo or records a clip — whichever of PHOTO / VIDEO is lit above it.

Point it at something moving. A still scene has nothing to trace.

No WebGL2

This needs WebGL2. On iPhone, update to iOS 16 or later and open the page in Safari.

Built-in filters custom

Numbered as in the row above the shutter; keys 0–9 pick the first ten. Filters marked + are layers: tap one to put it on top of the filter that is on, tap it again to take it off. A second motion filter replaces the first.

Your own filters

Start here

  1. Point the camera at something moving. A still scene gives the effect nothing to work with — wave a hand in front of it and you will see it immediately.
  2. Pick a filter from the row just above the shutter. 0 Off gives you the plain picture back. To layer ASCII, Negative or Thermal on top, open Options and use Filters.
  3. Zoom with two fingers on the picture, or tap 1× above the filters for 2× and 3×.
  4. Tap Freeze on the right. The picture stops refreshing but the motion keeps dragging it. Tap again to release.
  5. PHOTO or VIDEO, above the shutter, decides what the big button does. In VIDEO, tap once to start and again to stop.
  6. When a clip or a still is ready it appears as a bar at the top of the screen. On iPhone, use Save or share.

The three buttons on the right

Freeze holds the current picture as the reference, and everything after it is painted with that frozen frame, dragged by live motion. It is the strongest single control in the app.

Burst throws two seconds of violent breakage and releases on its own. Good on a beat.

Clear wipes the mess that has built up and starts again from the live picture.

What is actually happening

Compressed video stores a few whole pictures and describes everything between them as motion. Damage the whole pictures and the motion keeps playing over a frame it was never meant for — that is a datamosh.

This app estimates the motion itself, then withholds the refresh. Pixels stay where they are while the motion drags them around, which is the melting you see.

If something looks wrong

Nothing is tracing. The filter may be set to 0 Off, or the scene is too static. Wave your hand at the camera.

It stutters. Lower Detail under Input, or set Motion to the fast setting.

No camera at all. The page has to be on an https address. Opened from a file on disk, the camera stays blocked.

The flashlight button is missing. It only appears once a camera with a lamp is running — front cameras usually have none.

Keep it on your home screen

eltrace runs in the browser. There is nothing to download, and it works the same once added to the home screen.

iPhone and iPad. In Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.

Android. In Chrome, tap ⋮, then Add to Home screen (or Install app).

Computer. Bookmark it, or use Install in the browser menu if it offers one.

Open it from its https address, not from a saved file, or the camera stays blocked.

Keeping a filter

Under Filters you can name and save whatever you have dialled in, and Copy as a link packs every setting into a URL you can send to someone or keep as a bookmark.

On a keyboard: space play or pause, F freeze, B burst, C clear, R record, P photo, L flashlight, 0–9 filters 0–9, O options, Esc close.

Where the picture comes from

The test pattern needs no camera and no file. The camera needs a secure (https) page and your permission.

Sound

Camera clips use the microphone, a video file uses its own track. No gain, no noise suppression, no echo cancellation.
